Fingerprints

Computed from the letter's ink mask only, so paper tone, ink colour and neighbours do not matter:

  1. crop to the ink, pad to a square (aspect ratio kept, 1 px border), resize to 48 × 48 (area interpolation);
  2. Gaussian blur, σ = 1 px, so a stroke shifted by a pixel or two still matches;
  3. pixel part: the blurred image at 24 × 24 = 576 values, scaled to unit length;
  4. stroke-direction part (HOG): Sobel gradients; 6 × 6 cells of 8 px, each a 9-bin histogram of gradient direction (0–180°) weighted by strength; cells normalised in overlapping 2 × 2 blocks (L2, clipped at 0.2, renormalised): 5 × 5 blocks × 36 = 900 values, scaled to unit length;
  5. size part: width and height of the ink divided by the page's line spacing, × 0.5, so a dot and a big letter of similar shape stay apart;
  6. the whole vector (1,478 float32) is scaled to unit length, so the Euclidean distance between two fingerprints runs from 0 (same) to 2 (opposite).

The clustering

Plain NumPy; memory grows with the number of groups, not with pairs of letters. Done separately for letters, dandas and digits; red and black letters share groups.

  1. Leader pass (in reading order, batches of 1,024): a letter joins the nearest group centre within 0.55; otherwise it starts a new group. Centres are the unit-length mean of their members (a plain mean of many shapes is short and lies near everything, so a big group would swallow its neighbours).
  2. Merge: pairs of groups whose centres are closer than 0.55, closest first, are merged, but only if the merged group stays compact: 90 % of its members within 0.5 of the new centre. This stops groups creeping from a letter into its look-alikes.
  3. Reassign: every letter moves to its nearest centre. Merge and reassign repeat 3 times.
  4. Unsure: a letter further than 0.5 from its group's centre leaves it; groups of fewer than 2 letters are not groups. These letters are Unsure.

Groups are numbered by size (g0001 is the largest). Each letter keeps its distance to its group's centre (the review shows nearest first). Pages added later are not re-clustered: each new letter is offered the nearest group within 0.55 as a suggestion.

Tesseract's readings (printed books)

  1. The line image is turned into black ink on white by Otsu's threshold on its grey values, with a 10 px white border (the paper and its colour are gone before Tesseract sees it).
  2. Tesseract 5 (LSTM engine) runs as a separate program: model script/Devanagari, page segmentation mode 7 (one text line), output hOCR with a box per character (hocr_char_boxes=1) and the alternatives per character with their confidences (lstm_choice_mode=2).
  3. Aksharas: the characters are grouped into aksharas (consonants joined by virama with their nukta, vowel signs and marks; independent vowels; digits, dandas, ऽ and ॐ on their own).
  4. Matching aksharas to letters by x position only, with dynamic programming (like a text diff): steps are 1 akshara ↔ 1 letter, up to 3 aksharas on one letter (an uncut word), 1 akshara over up to 3 letters (a cut conjunct), or skipping either (cost 0.5). A step costs 1 − overlap, + 0.1 per extra item. Character boxes wider than 2.5 × the median are not trusted. A match is kept if the overlap is ≥ 0.8, or ≥ 0.6 and they are each other's best overlap; a line where fewer than half of its letters match is not used.
  5. Each matched letter stores its reading (a valid label, Devanagari NFC), the lowest confidence of its aksharas (0–100) and the alternatives.

Suggestions from other labelled books

For every letter, the 5 nearest labelled group centres of the chosen books (same fingerprint settings) within a fingerprint distance of 0.5 vote, each with weight 1 / distance. The reading is the winner; its confidence is its share of the vote (0–100). This is the first reader for handwriting.

From readings to a group suggestion

Suggestions are never stored: they are voted live from the readings of the group's current letters, so they follow every move and merge. The book's main reader votes: the letter reader if the book has read with it, then Tesseract (printed) or the other books (handwritten).

  • each reading weighs its confidence (at least 1); a group needs 3 read letters, and the winner 60 % of the weight; a tie gives nothing;
  • the second reader's suggestion is shown beside it ("both agree", or the other opinion);
  • labelled groups and rejected suggestions get none; Accept all ≥ 90 % accepts the groups whose winner has at least 90 %;
  • an unsure letter shows its own reading from confidence 80 (never Tesseract's on handwriting).

Splitting mixed groups

Shape alone cannot keep look-alikes (ने / ते) apart, so readings decide and shapes must agree: in a group, the letters read as another letter than the group's main reading leave it when there are at least 5 of them (and 10 % of the group), the centre of their fingerprints is at least 0.25 from the centre of the main reading's letters (a misreading of the same shape stays), and each is closer to its own reading's centre. Locked groups are left alone.

Labels at capture (printed, cut by Tesseract)

Right after capture: mixed groups are split as above; every unlabelled group whose vote gives a suggestion gets that label with status auto (not reviewed); groups with the same label are merged into the largest; an unsure letter whose reading is a group's label and whose fingerprint is within 0.55 of that group's centre joins it.

Review and storage

  • A group's label is stored in Devanagari NFC (label_dev) and shown in Gujarati; its status is auto (given by a reader), reviewed or labelled (by you).
  • Every review change (move, new group, merge, dissolve, label, accept, split, delete, join, crop) is one database transaction recorded in action with the state of every touched letter and group before and after; undo writes the before-state back, redo the after-state. Locked groups refuse every change.
  • After each change the touched groups' centres and their letters' distances are recomputed.
