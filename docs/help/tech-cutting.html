1. Preparing the page

  1. Colour space: the image is converted to CIE Lab (OpenCV, 0–255 per channel). L is lightness, a* is the red–green axis.
  2. Paper vs scanner: pixels darker than 0.45 × the 75th percentile of L that are connected to the image edge (8-connected components) are the scanner background. Ink is dark too but never touches the edge.
  3. Local paper tone: for L and for a*, a median filter of 151 px (full-page pixels) on a copy at 25 % size, then resized back. Strokes are a minority in every window, so the median follows the paper, including stains and uneven lighting.
  4. Ink masks (per pixel, against the local paper):
    • red ink: a* at least 14 above the paper's a* and L at least 8 darker;
    • black ink: not red and L below 0.62 × the paper's L.
    This is a local, relative threshold: the paper's own tone is the reference everywhere, so yellowed or stained paper does not become ink.
  5. Ruled lines: morphological opening with a vertical line of 15 % of the page height and a horizontal one of 15 % of the width (after widening by 5 px for slant). Only long straight runs survive; they are removed from both ink masks. A horizontal run with letters hanging from it (ink in the 22 px below it, 3 px down, in at least half of its columns) is the headline of a long printed word and stays.
  6. Text block: column and row ink profiles, smoothed over 41 px; the longest run of columns with at least 20 % of a typical text column's ink (gaps up to 60 px allowed), then the same for rows (5 %), plus a 15 px margin. Margin numbers and tape marks fall outside.
  7. Side borders: faint or broken border lines are not long enough to be removed as ruled lines, and used to end up inside the text block, cut as a "letter" at the end of every line. A lighter ink mask (L below 0.8 × the paper's L) is widened by 5 px and its breaks of up to 6 px are bridged; a column whose ink then runs for 12 % of the page height is a border (text strokes stop between lines: under 7 %). The innermost such column on each side, outside the block or up to 150 px inside its edge, limits the block: nothing beyond it is cut. A page without such a border keeps its block. The debug image draws the borders as orange lines.
  8. Specks: ink components smaller than 8 px (black) or 5 px (red) are removed.

2. Finding the lines

  1. Headline evidence: morphological opening with a 1 × 25 px horizontal element keeps only horizontal ink runs of 25 px or more (the shirorekha); stems, dandas and matras drop out.
  2. Line pitch: the autocorrelation of the row profile of those runs; its highest peak between 40 and 300 px is the line spacing (about 107 px on the printed 1933 book).
  3. Headline rows: local maxima of the smoothed row profile, strongest first, at least 0.6 × pitch apart; peaks weaker than 0.25 × a typical peak are dropped.
  4. Tracing each headline: in half-overlapping windows of 150 px, starting from the strongest window and walking left and right, each window searches within 0.3 × pitch of the previous one for its busiest row (at least 25 % of its columns inked). Then a polynomial (degree 2, or 1 for short lines) is fitted and windows further than 0.08 × pitch from it are replaced by the fit (they locked onto dandas or big matras). This follows slanted scans and wavy lines. Two traces that end on the same headline are one line.
  5. Main zone: the depth below the headline where the aligned ink drops under 25 % of the letter-body ink.
  6. Boundaries: between two lines, per window, the middle of the emptiest rows between 0.8 × main zone below the upper headline and the lower headline.
  7. Giving ink to lines: each 8-connected ink blob is given whole: touching one headline (±4 px) → that line; touching two → split at the boundary; touching none (anusvara, dots, loose matras) → the nearer of "just above the headline below" (an upper mark) and "just below the main zone above" (a lower mark).
  8. Line image: the crop with a 6 px margin; ink of other lines inside it is grown by 4 px and filled in from the surrounding paper (Telea inpainting, radius 5), so only this line's ink is left. The colours of the page are kept: a line image is not binarised.

3. Stroke pieces (first cut)

The headline is drawn letter by letter, so between two letters it either has a gap or thins sharply. For every column of the line, the headline thickness is the ink in a band from 8 px above to 4 px below the traced headline.

  • A break is a run of columns where the thickness is at most 0.5 × the line's typical thickness (soft limit) and somewhere at most 0.3 × (hard limit): hysteresis, because a join is often 1 px at its thinnest but a few px wide at the soft limit. Breaks narrower than 2 px are pen texture.
  • Inside a break the cut goes through every empty column run of the main zone, otherwise through the column with the least ink. A danda standing in a gap becomes a piece of its own.
  • Each piece takes the ink from 0.1 × pitch above the headline to the bottom of the main zone; upper and lower marks wait for step 4. Pieces with less than 15 px of ink (10 for red) are specks. Each piece records its headline width and its stem width (median row width in the lower main zone).

The line is a label image: every ink pixel carries the number of its letter. Pieces start as their own labels; the rules join them (union–find), split them and attach the rest.

5a. Printed: cutting by Tesseract's reading

Chosen per book (Pages & capture → Cutting into letters → By Tesseract's reading). Lines are found as above; Tesseract reads each line image and the line is cut into exactly the aksharas it read (an akshara is a consonant cluster with its vowel signs and marks, so conjuncts, reph and matras stay together):

  • between two aksharas, the cut goes to the column with the least ink, leaving out the headline rows, within 0.3 × the median akshara width of Tesseract's boundary (its boxes are rough);
  • cuts closer than 0.3 × that width are not made: those aksharas stay one sample with their text together (a "word" sample);
  • each letter keeps the akshara(s) it was cut as, stored as its Tesseract reading;
  • a line Tesseract cannot read, or reads as fewer than half as many aksharas as the shape cut finds letters, keeps the shape cut.

5b. Printed: the ા bar fix

Both cuts sometimes give an aa bar to the next letter (नार as न + ार). A sample starts with a stray bar when, in its mask: the first ink below the headline is a tall stroke (ink in ≥ 75 % of the rows), at most 0.3 × the letter width; then a gap (ink in ≤ 5 % of the rows) and a letter body of ≥ 0.4 × the letter width; and the letter before ends within 0.4 × the letter width on the same line, without a bar of its own. The bar then moves back. An i-hook (ि, written before its letter, with a mark arching over) is never moved; the e sign of the next letter is told from it by its shape (its left end stays 0.35 × headline height above) and by Tesseract's reading. Handwriting is left alone.

6. What each letter becomes

  • Letter image (letters/<page>/L01_003.png): the crop from the original page with a 4 px margin; neighbouring letters' ink inside it is inpainted (Telea, radius 3). Colours are kept.
  • Mask (L01_003_mask.png): the letter's own ink pixels, black and white. Grouping and the letter reader work on the mask only, never on the colours.
  • Fingerprint: see part 2.
  • Pages run in parallel worker processes (up to 6); a page that fails is recorded and never stops the others.
