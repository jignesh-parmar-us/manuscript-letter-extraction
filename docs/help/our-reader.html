How our reader reads

Text → Whole lines (our reader) explained in plain words, with examples from the printed Vachnamrut (book 6): how a line becomes text, what the language model and the letter reader do, what it learns from, and how it is measured.

Two readers of the same line

Tesseract (Whole lines (Tesseract)) A ready-made reader for printed Devanagari. It has never seen this book; it reads each line image as it is. It is the text you correct by default.
Our reader (Whole lines (our reader)) Learned only from this book: your reviewed groups and your checked lines. It reads the same lines without Tesseract, and does not need Tesseract to have read the page first. Where the two read differently (when Tesseract has read it too), the aksharas are highlighted in both tabs.

A correction or a ✓ belongs to the line, not to a reader: it is the same in both tabs, and every ✓ line becomes a checked line that both readers are measured on.

Not to be confused: Training Data → Readers trains another letter reader. That one reads letters that are already cut out (Cut letters) and finds the possible mix-ups in your groups. Our reader has its own letter reader inside, trained with Retrain, because it has to look at pieces of a whole line, including pieces that are not letters at all.

How our reader reads a line, step by step

Example: the line on Untitled-26 that has पग उपर्य पगने (“leg over leg”). Tesseract reads उपरय there; the र्य of this print is wide and flat, a र beside a य.

0 · The line's ink
The line's cut letters are pasted back together into one black-and-white picture of the line. A page border caught at the line's edge (thin, broken, taller than the letters, as on Untitled-51 and 52) is removed first; a danda (। ॥) is solid and no taller than the letters, so it stays.
↓
1 · Places where a letter could start or end
A place every 9 pixels along the line, plus every gap between letters and every thin point under the headline. About 300 places on a long line.
↓
2 · Windows
Every stretch between two places that is between 0.2 and 2.4 times as wide as a usual letter is a window: about 7,600 per line. Each window is cut to its ink and turned into numbers (its features: the picture at 20 × 20, the directions of its strokes, its size).

A few of the windows around उपर्य.

3 · The letter reader looks at every window
For each window it gives how likely each letter is, and how likely it is not a letter (half a letter, two letters, a letter with its neighbour). For the wide window above, a reader that has learned this wide र्य gives र्य most of its probability and the other letters and “not a letter” little. Windows whose best letter is unlikely are dropped (about 2,300 of the 7,600 are kept).
↓
4 · The search: the best way to cut the whole line
There are millions of ways to cut a line into letters. The search walks from left to right and keeps the ten best ways so far. Each letter on a way adds up: how sure the letter reader is, a bonus per letter, a penalty when the window is much wider or narrower than that letter usually is, and the language model's opinion (below).

5 · The text
The letters of the best way, with a space where a gap is wide, and each akshara's place on the page (so you can click it). About 7 seconds per line.

The language model (not a reader)

The language model never looks at the ink. It only knows which aksharas follow each other in this book: from the labels of your Training Data pages in reading order, the lines you proofread in Cut letters, and your word lists (Read Book → Dictionary). Your checked lines are not in it (yet): they teach the letter reader. For two or three aksharas in a row it knows how often the next one comes.

Example (from your book's language model): after भग it has seen वा (भगवान) nearly every time and बा practically never, so when the letter reader is unsure between वा and बा there, it tips the choice to वा. Where the shape is clear, the letter reader wins.

The letter reader: linear or neural network

Both turn a window's features (about 1,300 numbers) into a score for every letter.

Linear reader (used now) Every letter has one weight per feature: “ink here makes क more likely, ink there makes it less likely”. The scores are sums. Simple, fast, and hard to fool with little data.
Neural network reader First 384 “combinations” of features (for example “a hook on top and a bar on the right”), then the letters from those. It can learn more complicated shapes, but needs more examples and trains several times slower.

On your book the neural network has not been better: 7.50% against 7.32% for the linear reader (both learning from your corrections). So the linear one stays.

Why vowel signs are the hardest: नी and नो, री and रो, पा and पो differ by a small mark, but a window is shrunk to 20 × 20 pixels, so the mark gets only a few pixels. Close-ups of the top strip, the bottom strip and the right side of each window are being tried (see TUNING_PHASE2.md).

Every label counts the same in total, however many letters it has: वा with 195 letters does not outweigh बा with 1. What matters is that the letters in a group are right, and that there are real examples of each shape.

Retrain, Read and Measure

Retrain (about half an hour)
Learns again from your reviewed groups and every checked line. It does not read: the pages keep their reading until you read them again. The tab says when your groups or checked lines changed since it learned.
Read all pages / Read unread pages / Read this page (about 7 s per line)
Reads with the reader as it is. New pages need no retraining. Read unread pages reads only the pages our reader has not read yet (the number on the button), and leaves the rest as they are.
Measure (about 2 hours)
Scores our reader on your checked lines, beside Tesseract. It must not score a page the reader learned from (that would be like marking an exam the pupil has seen), and every Read Book page is also a Training Data page. So the pages with checked lines are split into 4 groups; for each group a reader is trained without those pages (and without their checked lines) and reads them. That is four trainings, hence the time. The letters' features are kept between runs (see below), so a Measure after a few changes takes much less than the first.
Quick measure (about half a Measure)
Only 2 of the 4 groups of pages. It is there to throw out a bad idea early, and nothing more: its numbers are marked screening only and must never be quoted as a result; judge a change on the full Measure. It does not replace the last Measure; both are shown, the quick one while it is the newer.

Photos and scans at another size

The reader learned letters about 67 px high (line spacing about 106 px). A photo of a whole card, with letters 25–30 px high, used to be read almost entirely wrong, even though its coloured background is removed correctly. Now our reader enlarges (or shrinks) the line to the size it learned at before it reads, when the page's line spacing differs by more than 20%. On 7 checked lines of two WhatsApp photos the characters wrong fell from 80.5% to 10.7% (Tesseract: 8.5%). Pages of the usual size are read as before. Letters cut from such a small photo are still poor training samples; a sharper, larger picture is better still.

“The cache” (in the experiments, not in the app)

Trying one idea fairly means four trainings and reading 99 lines: about two hours. Most of that time goes into computing the features of the training letters and of every window of every checked line, and those are the same for every idea about the reader or the search. So for the experiments they are computed once per group of pages and kept in files (about 5 GB), a cache; then a new idea, such as another language model weight, takes minutes instead of hours.

Since 16 October the app keeps a cache of its own, in line-search/features/ in the book folder: each training letter's features (about 14 rows of numbers per letter: the letter, its window on the line, the window shifted a little, its varied copies and its "not a letter" pieces) and every letter mask in one file. Retrain and Measure compute only the features of letters that changed (a new page, a recut line, a changed mask); reviewing or relabelling a group changes nothing there, since the labels are always read from your groups. It is about 0.5 GB for book 6. It is only a cache: deleting the folder is safe, it is made again (the next Retrain or Measure then takes as long as the first). The random choices for a letter (its shifts and varied copies) now come from the letter itself, so the same letter gets the same rows in every group of pages and every run.

What else the app keeps is the trained reader (line-search/reader.npz), the last measure (line-search/measure.json; a quick one in measure-quick.json) and each line's reading in the library.
