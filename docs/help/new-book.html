Adding a new book

How to turn a folder of page images into clean letters with clean labels, with as little hand work as possible, and get it ready for training the letter checker and our reader.

New here, or lost? The steps in order shows the whole route as a flowchart, from creating a book to downloading its text. The parts explained (and How our reader reads, for Whole lines (our reader)) says what Training Data, groups, the letter checker, the language model and the Reading setup each are. How it works explains each step technically: the algorithms, scores and image processing.

1. Before you start

  • The page images (JPG, PNG, TIFF or BMP) or a PDF, in page order by name (page2 comes before page10). When you make a dataset or a book you upload them (or, for a dataset, copy a folder): choose the files, or drag them from Finder onto the box (the images, not their folder). The app keeps the copies in its uploads folder (shown at the bottom of the Books and Datasets screens; change… moves it: the pages uploaded before are copied into the new folder, each copy checked, and used from there; the old folder is left as it is, and nothing changes if a copy fails). Your originals are never changed. A folder can also be used where it is (Pages & capture / Prepare).
  • Datasets and books from before the uploads folder read their pages from a folder of your own. The first time the app starts with this version, it copies those pages into the uploads folder (one copy per dataset or book, also when several use the same folder; on a Mac the copies take no extra disk space), checks every copy against its original, and then uses the copies. It backs up the library database first. A notice on the Books and Datasets screens says what was moved, where the backup is, and which of your folders no dataset or book uses any more: you can delete those when you like, because the app never deletes them. A dataset or book whose folder or a page image is missing keeps its folder and is tried again at the next start. Your groups, labels and line truth do not change.
  • Printed or handwritten? This decides how the letters are cut and who suggests labels: printed books are read by Tesseract; handwritten books learn from the books you have already labelled.
  • For printed books, Tesseract must be installed (see docs/INSTALL_TESSERACT.md). The Review groups section says so if it is missing.
  • Keep your older labelled books until the new book has learned from them (section 6): their labels are the fastest way to label the new one.

2. A printed book printed

About 10 minutes of waiting for 20 to 25 pages, and a few clicks. Expect about a quarter of the letters to be labelled right after capture, and more after step 4 if you have other labelled books.

  1. Create the dataset Datasets (top right) → New dataset: a name, Writing Printed, and the pages: Upload page images or a PDF (choose several images, or one PDF: its pages become images), or Copy a folder of page images. Create dataset opens Pages & capture. Or choose Later and then Pages & capture → Folder with the page images → Use this folder: a folder used where it is (only read, never changed; Change… points the dataset at a moved folder).
  2. Choose the cutting before the first capture Pages & capture → Cutting into letters → By Tesseract's reading.
    Each line is cut into the letters Tesseract reads: about half as many letters left joined as with the shape cut.
  3. Capture Capture letters, then wait (about 4 to 5 minutes for 23 pages). ા bars cut onto the next letter (નાર as ન + ાર) are given back to their letter.
    At the end, every letter has Tesseract's reading, groups that mixed two letters are split, and the groups Tesseract is sure about are labelled, marked not reviewed.
  4. Learn from your other labelled books Review groups → Details: tick the labelled books of the same kind of print, then Suggest from labelled books (a few seconds).
    Each letter gets the label of the closest group you labelled there. Where Tesseract and your books agree, the group's suggestion says so.
  5. Split mixed groups Split mixed groups, once.
    Letters read as another letter and shaped differently move to a group of their own. A group of one letter is never split by its strokes.
  6. Accept the sure suggestions Accept N with ≥ 90 %. One Undo takes it all back.
  7. Review as in section 4.
Tip: books cut by Tesseract do not need Fix cuts with Tesseract: their cuts already follow its reading. Fix cuts is for printed books cut by the shapes of the ink.

3. A handwritten book handwritten

Tesseract reads handwriting poorly (about a third of the letters wrong), so the labels come from you and from your other handwritten books.

  1. Create the dataset Datasets → New dataset, Writing Handwritten, and upload the page images (or copy their folder). The cutting stays By the shapes of the ink.
  2. Capture Capture letters.
  3. If you have another labelled handwritten book in the same hand Review groups → Suggest from labelled books, then Split mixed groups and Accept N with ≥ 90 %.
  4. Otherwise, label the biggest groups by hand Sort the group list by size and label the largest 30 to 50 groups: they hold most of the letters. Use Letters… or type in Gujarati or Devanagari.
    These labels are what the letter checker (C14) and our reader learn this hand from, and what the next handwritten book will learn from.
  5. Unsure Accept the suggested groups (→) for the letters that clearly belong to one.

4. Reviewing the labels

Labels given by Tesseract or by other books are marked not reviewed. Check them in this order; the first steps do the most good. You do not need to finish every group.

5. Training the letter checker

Training Data → Letter checker → Train teaches the letter checker your reviewed letters (about a minute). It finds letters that are probably in the wrong group (Check these) and suggests labels; it does not read books (that is our reader, section 7). Train again whenever you have reviewed or corrected more: each training makes a new checker, and older ones are kept. (Until October 2026 this section was called Readers, and the checker a letter reader.)

  • Not every label is needed. It learns only from labelled letters; labels with fewer letters than the minimum you choose (5 by default, down to 1) are left out and listed. Its report shows how many letters of pages it did not learn from it reads right, and the accuracy of every letter, so you see which ones need more samples.
  • Wrong labels are the problem. A group labelled ने that still holds ते letters teaches the checker that both are ने. That is why the review matters more than the number of labels.
  • It learns only from groups you reviewed, unless you tick also groups not reviewed.
  • Check these (Review groups, side bar) lists the letters the checker is sure are in the wrong group: Move to …, or It is right if the checker is wrong. The same letters are outlined in their group as possible mix-ups, with the letter each reads as (the list on the left shows ⚠ and a count; Show → Possible mix-ups lists those groups): Select the possible mix-ups, then Move to their likely letters or Not a mix-up. Fix them, then train again (the list is from the last training).
  • The checker also votes in the hidden Cut letters view (section 7), on the letters it was taught. The text of a book comes from Whole lines (Tesseract or our reader), not from the checker.
  • Good enough to start: the big groups reviewed, and the mixed ones split.

6. Replacing old books

  1. Make the new book first and run Suggest from labelled books with the old books ticked, so their labels reach the new one.
  2. Accept and review in the new book (sections 2 and 4).
  3. Then delete the old books Datasets → Delete. This removes the dataset's letters, groups and labels in the library, and the pages uploaded into it; pages in a folder of your own are never touched. It cannot be undone.
Tip: keep at least one labelled book of each kind (one printed, one handwritten): new books of the same kind learn from it.

7. Reading the whole book (Read Book)

Everything above happens in a dataset: sample pages whose letters are cut, grouped, reviewed and labelled. What is read into text is a book, a separate thing on its own Books screen (since October 2026; before, one book held both parts). A book learns from a dataset: our reader learns that dataset's reviewed letters, and the language model its labels. Make one on the Books screen → New book: Writing, a name, Learns from (a dataset of the same writing, or none yet), and its pages: several page images or one PDF (Page images or a PDF). Create book uploads them and opens Prepare; a PDF's pages become images there (the job shows its progress). The book's header links its dataset; a dataset lists the books that use it.

Tip: a printed book read only by Tesseract (Text → Whole lines) needs no dataset: choose no dataset yet and start here.
  1. Book → Prepare. The pages uploaded when the book was made are here. More page images can be added with Upload page images or a PDF → Upload (also to a book read from a PDF: they are added to its pages; not to a book whose pages are in a folder of your own: put the images into that folder, and they show up as new pages). Instead of uploading you can type the path of the book's PDF and click Use this PDF (its pages become images in the book folder), or choose a folder of page images used where it is.
  2. The pages list (under Prepare, the same in a dataset's Pages & capture) shows every page in its order, with a thumbnail (Thumbnails hides them), its status (Prepared, Not prepared yet, Excluded) and how many checked lines, corrections or reviewed letters it has. ▲ / ▼, or dragging a page by its handle ⠿ to another place, change the order; reading and the downloads follow it. New pages come after the last one once you have changed the order (before that, in file-name order). Exclude keeps a page with everything on it but skips it: a book's page is not prepared, read, measured, trained on or downloaded; a dataset's page is not captured, trained on or exported (Include brings it back). A page not prepared yet can be excluded too, for example a cover. Delete removes the page with its lines, readings, text, checked lines and corrections; an uploaded image or a PDF's page is removed too, but an image in a folder of your own stays there and the page stays in the list, excluded. Excluding or deleting a page with checked lines, corrections or reviewed letters asks first and says what is on it.
  3. Prepare for reading. The lines of every page are found (and their letters cut: our reader needs their ink) in the background, a few pages at a time; a prepared page says Prepared. Stopping keeps the pages already prepared; the next Prepare the remaining pages continues. Prepare again does one page again, for example after a failure.
  4. Book pages shows each page with its letters, to fix cuts (draw a box, join, split, delete). Read Book letters are never grouped automatically; only the letters you correct in the Text view (step 6) go into groups.
  5. A page that reads badly can no longer be moved into the dataset (Use for training is gone since datasets and books are separate); copying a book's pages into a dataset is planned for later. For now, add the page image to the dataset's folder and use Add new pages there.
  1. Text: choose Whole lines (Tesseract) or Whole lines (our reader) at the top. Whole lines (the best on the checked lines): Read all pages (or Read unread pages: only the pages not read yet, e.g. after adding pages); Tesseract reads each line image as it is, no letters are cut; With the language model applies the corrections of the language model (Read Book → Reading setup; underlined). Whole page shows the page As a page (the page image, its text below) or as Line strips (every line as its strip of the page with its text under it); Line by line shows one line at a time (◀ ▶). Aligned text puts each akshara under its letter (untick it for plain text). Click an akshara to see it on the page; ← → move between aksharas, ↑ ↓ between lines. ✎ Correct a line and Save; ✓ marks it reviewed (a reviewed line is also a checked line in Line truth). Whole lines (our reader) (it needs only the prepared pages; Tesseract need not have read them): Read all pages: our reader (the line search) reads the same lines without Tesseract, from this book's reviewed letters (the first time it learns them, a few minutes; then about 7 s per line). Its text is shown in the same views; the aksharas where the two readers differ (the second opinion: the places most likely to be wrong) are highlighted in both tabs, while the text of each tab stays that reader's own, and clicking one shows what the other read. On the checked lines the marks fall on about a fifth of the aksharas and catch 88% of Tesseract's remaining mistakes. When groups were reviewed or changed since it learned, it says so: Retrain. Retrain learns from your reviewed groups and every checked line (it finds which ink is which akshara of your text, so it also learns letters no group has yet; about half an hour); it does not read the pages: Read all pages (or a page) afterwards, and new pages can be read without retraining. Measure scores it on your checked lines, fairly (trained again without the pages it is scored on, about 2 hours), beside Tesseract, with every line listed. A correction or ✓ belongs to the line: the same in both tabs. Download as .txt, Word, PDF, a picture of the text per page, or the page images (PDF), with uncertain letters as [?], [?ક] or the best guess. The button before them chooses the pages: with none ticked, every page (All 312 pages); click it for a tick box per page, Select all, Select none and Only this page. The ticks are kept for the book in this browser. A font of your own (+ beside ગુજરાતી / देवनागरी): upload a .ttf / .otf font or pick one from Font Maker; it becomes a tab for every book, and the text, the PDF, the pictures and the Word file (with the font embedded when it allows it) use it. Cut letters (the older way: the page cut into letters, read by Tesseract and checked with your groups and letter checker; it reads worse than whole lines) is hidden; its data is kept. To bring it back for experiments, tick Show Cut letters in the book's Prepare → Settings. There: Convert all pages (or one page); Tesseract only shows what Tesseract read, Tesseract + your groups adds your groups' check. Uncertain letters are highlighted on both, and clicking one shows it on the page and what each reader read.
  2. Proofread letters (Cut letters only): click a wrong letter (or Next uncertain ▶) and type the right one, then Correct. The correction labels that letter, so it shows in Review groups and the next reader training learns from it; Undo takes it back. Two letters… splits a box (click where the letters meet), Join with the next joins two boxes, Not a letter deletes a speck. Click ✓ at the end of a line once its text is right: converting again keeps proofread lines, and the proofread lines measure how many characters the conversion gets wrong.
  3. Line truth: type what a few lines really say (Tesseract's reading of the whole line is filled in: correct it and press Enter). 30 to 50 lines from a few pages are the test set every way of reading is measured on, independent of how the letters were cut.
  4. Reading setup (the Dictionary tab before; its old address opens here) has three parts. 1. Datasets used: the datasets whose reviewed letters our reader learns and whose labels the language model learns, one or several: Add dataset (one of another writing is allowed, with a warning), Remove from this book (the dataset itself is untouched), Open, New dataset…. A dataset can only be deleted on the Datasets screen, which says which books use it. 2. Word lists and the language model: the language model corrects some uncertain letters from the text it knows (underlined in the Text view, still marked uncertain). It learns from the datasets' labels and your proofread lines; Import a UTF-8 text file in Gujarati or Devanagari (the full text of the book, or works like it) to give it much more, then read again. Untick Use the language model to turn it off. It follows the datasets and word lists by itself. 3. Reading model: what our reader was trained on, out of date when the datasets, their reviewed letters or the checked lines changed since, Train / Retrain and Measure (fold-fair on the checked lines: each group of tested pages is left out of the training, also where the same page is in a dataset under another name; takes a while). Each book has its own reading model, kept in models/ in the library folder.
Printed books are read by Tesseract and checked against your labelled groups; handwritten books by your groups alone for now, so their text is rough until many groups are reviewed. Every letter you correct teaches the next reader training.
