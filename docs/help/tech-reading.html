4 · Reading a book into text

Read Book pages are cut like Training Data pages but never grouped. Converting reads every cut letter with up to three readers, decides each letter, and lets the language model correct the uncertain ones.

3a. Tesseract on each line

  1. The line image (page colours, other lines inpainted away) is binarised by Otsu's threshold on grey values: black ink on white, a 10 px white border, not scaled (letters are about 70 px tall at 300 dpi).
  2. Tesseract 5 LSTM, script/Devanagari, --psm 7, hOCR with character boxes and alternatives (lines run in parallel threads).
  3. Its aksharas are aligned to the cut letters by the dynamic programming of part 2; each matched letter gets (text, confidence 0–100).
  4. Gap filling: a letter the alignment left without a reading (or every letter of a line the alignment refused) takes the characters whose box centre lies inside its x span, with the lowest of their confidences. Measured on the printed book: character error rate 13.1 % → 10.8 %.

3b and 3c. Your groups and your reader

  • Groups: the letter's fingerprint against the centres of the book's labelled groups; the nearest (label, distance) is the groups' reading, valid within 0.55.
  • Reader: the active reader's features and probabilities (part 3): its best 3 labels. It only knows the labels it was trained on.

5. The language model

  • Model: akshara trigrams with Witten-Bell smoothing (back-off to bigrams and single aksharas; add-one for an akshara never seen). Learned from the book's Training Data labels in reading order (a stretch ends at an unlabelled letter), its proofread lines and the word lists it uses. Spaces are left out. Rebuilt when any of these changes.
  • Candidates: a sure letter has only its reading (never changed). An uncertain one gets its reading plus what the readers saw: Tesseract's text weighted by its confidence / 100, the reader's best 3 by probability, the two nearest groups within 0.55 by 0.5 × (1 − distance); normalised to sum to 1.
  • Search: beam search over the line (20 states, each the last two aksharas) maximising Σ [1.0 × log P(akshara | previous two) + log candidate weight].
  • Safety: each changed letter is checked alone: the line's score with the change must beat the score without it by at least 3 (log probability, a factor of about 20). A changed letter stays uncertain and keeps what it was (lm_from), shown underlined.

6. Line text and what is stored

  • Letters in reading order (x); printed books get a space where the gap to the previous letter is wider than 0.5 × the line's median letter width; handwriting gets none.
  • Gujarati by the fixed mapping; downloads write uncertain letters as [?], [?ક] or the plain guess.
  • page_text keeps every letter with its sample, box, text, confidence, source, readings and, separately, Tesseract's own decision (for "Tesseract only"); files in text/read/<page>.txt and .json.

7. Proofreading and line truth

  • The text shown is the stored reading brought up to date with the samples: a letter whose sample is in a labelled group shows the label (your correction); letters whose sample was split, joined or deleted give way to the new samples. Correcting a letter labels its sample (an undoable action), which puts it in Training Data.
  • A proofread line keeps its text; converting again keeps its letters.
  • Line truth is separate: what a whole line says, typed by you from Tesseract's whole-line reading (no cutting at all), kept per line. It is the test set for comparing ways of reading, because it cannot be biased by the cuts.

Would removing the background help?

The background is already removed before every engine.

EngineWhat it gets
Grouping, letter readerthe letter's ink mask only (black and white, from the page's local paper-relative threshold); paper, stains and colour never reach them
Tesseractthe line image with other lines' ink inpainted away, then hard-binarised by Otsu: pure black on white

So removing the background in itself would change nothing. What may matter is how the image is cleaned, and these can be measured on your line truth:

  • Hard binarisation can hurt Tesseract. Its LSTM models were trained on grey, anti-aliased text. A hard black-and-white image with one global Otsu threshold per line loses faint strokes (broken letters) or fattens thick ones (closed loops). A flattened grey image (the page divided by its local paper tone, as in step 1 of part 1) keeps the background white and the strokes' edges soft.
  • Scale. Lines go to Tesseract unscaled, with letters about 70 px tall; Tesseract's models often do better on smaller text. Scaling the letter bodies to 30–40 px (ocr_letter_height, already a setting) is a cheap test.
  • The border "|" marks. Page border rules that survive in a line image are read as "|"; removing them from the line image before Tesseract would help directly.
  • Denoising and stroke repair (small opening / closing, speck removal at the line level) can help faded print, or hurt fine matras.

All four can be compared on your checked lines, line by line, once enough lines are checked.
